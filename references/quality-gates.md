# Quality Gates

## Requirement Gate

- The prompt satisfies the user's subject, story beat, duration, aspect, language, style, and audio request.
- Any supplied reference has one explicit role.
- Unknown platform limits are not presented as confirmed facts.

## Scene Gate

- The shot has one primary job.
- The action visibly changes across time.
- The content load is achievable within the duration.
- The ending is stable and useful.

## Directing Gate

- Blocking and camera make the scene job readable.
- Subject motion and camera motion do not compete without purpose.
- Light has a source, direction, quality, and narrative function.
- Dialogue, ambience, synchronized effects, music, and silence are intentional.

## Timing Gate

- The first interval starts at 0.
- Intervals are ordered, contiguous, and non-overlapping.
- The final interval ends at the requested duration.
- Each interval contains a causal action progression, not a static restatement.

## Continuity Gate

- Continuous shots use an inspected real result, not the planned ending.
- The opening inherits observable pose, action phase, axis, light, environment, props, and audio tail.
- Completed actions are not replayed.
- Motivated cuts preserve relevant story state without forcing identical framing.
- Independent shots are not unnecessarily blocked by prior results.

## Purity Gate

- The delivered prompt contains only executable model input.
- There are no headings, explanations, status notes, internal IDs, filenames, paths, or copying instructions.
- Failure-prevention instructions are concrete and short.
- The output does not claim media was inspected when it was unavailable.

## Revision Gate

- The observed failure is identified before rewriting.
- Working requirements remain intact.
- Changes address the failure rather than expanding the prompt indiscriminately.
