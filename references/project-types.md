# Video Project Types

Use this reference to choose structure and production emphasis. Hybrid projects may combine two patterns, but one format should lead.

## Contents

- Narrative short or scene
- Commercial or product film
- Corporate, tourism, or institutional promo
- Music video or visual poem
- Trailer, teaser, or announcement
- Documentary or branded documentary
- Explainer, tutorial, or educational video
- Social short or vertical video
- World, event, or atmosphere showcase
- Runtime heuristics

## 1. Narrative Short Or Scene

Primary goal: dramatic change.

Typical structure:

1. establish person, place, and immediate want
2. introduce obstacle or irreversible event
3. escalate through choices rather than exposition
4. force a decisive action
5. resolve the scene while leaving an emotional afterimage

Prioritize character blocking, causality, dialogue subtext, screen direction, geography, and state continuity. Deliver a scene script plus shot list and performance notes.

## 2. Commercial Or Product Film

Primary goal: make a specific audience believe or do something.

Typical structure:

1. attention hook
2. recognizable problem or aspiration
3. product reveal and mechanism
4. proof, transformation, or demonstration
5. brand memory and call to action

Prioritize product truth, legibility, brand rules, legal copy, pack shots, benefit hierarchy, and platform-safe duration. Avoid cinematic imagery that obscures the product.

## 3. Corporate, Tourism, Or Institutional Promo

Primary goal: communicate capability, identity, trust, or place.

Typical structure:

1. identity and scale
2. people and process
3. capability or cultural proof
4. future-facing synthesis
5. clear closing statement

Prioritize authentic operations, real locations, representative people, factual accuracy, and restrained claims. Build a location and activity matrix before the shot list.

## 4. Music Video Or Visual Poem

Primary goal: create a visual-emotional system around music or rhythm.

Typical structure:

1. visual motif introduction
2. motif variation and world expansion
3. rhythmic or emotional escalation
4. bridge, rupture, or tonal inversion
5. return, release, or final iconic image

Prioritize beat mapping, repeated visual motifs, performance coverage, match transitions, color progression, and editorial rhythm. If music is unavailable, design timing with neutral beat markers rather than inventing copyrighted lyrics or melody.

## 5. Trailer, Teaser, Or Announcement

Primary goal: create curiosity and a memorable promise without exhausting the story.

Typical structure:

1. cold-open question or striking premise
2. controlled reveals
3. acceleration montage
4. silence, reversal, or signature line
5. title and release information

Prioritize escalation, reveal order, clean audio punctuations, title-safe frames, and spoiler control. A teaser may omit explanation; a trailer still needs a comprehensible promise.

## 6. Documentary Or Branded Documentary

Primary goal: reveal reality through evidence, testimony, and observation.

Typical structure:

1. human or factual question
2. context and stakes
3. testimony, process, or competing view
4. discovery or reframing
5. grounded conclusion or open question

Prioritize source reliability, interview intent, B-roll coverage, releases, archival provenance, and clear separation of fact, inference, and reconstruction. Do not fabricate quotes.

## 7. Explainer, Tutorial, Or Educational Video

Primary goal: transfer understanding or enable an action.

Typical structure:

1. outcome and prerequisite
2. mental model
3. ordered steps or mechanism
4. worked example
5. recap, caveat, and next action

Prioritize information hierarchy, on-screen readability, step completeness, demonstrations, error states, and pacing that permits comprehension. Use graphics only when they clarify a relationship.

## 8. Social Short Or Vertical Video

Primary goal: earn attention quickly and complete one idea on a small screen.

Typical structure:

1. first-frame signal or spoken hook
2. immediate proof or tension
3. compact progression
4. payoff
5. loop, comment prompt, or call to action

Prioritize first-second comprehension, vertical-safe staging, subtitles, mobile text size, clear faces or products, and minimal narrative overhead. Avoid treating every social video as a fast-cut advertisement.

## 9. World, Event, Or Atmosphere Showcase

Primary goal: communicate scale, variety, and internal coherence.

Typical structure:

1. orienting overview
2. signature location or culture
3. people, craft, trade, food, and ordinary life
4. transport, ecology, conflict, or spectacle
5. unifying image or shared ritual

Prioritize geography, cultural contrast, daily life, material culture, transport, food, entertainment, ecology, and recurring motifs. Use a world matrix so spectacle does not erase lived detail.

## Runtime Heuristics

- Under 15 seconds: one idea, one turn, one memory image.
- 15-30 seconds: hook, demonstration or escalation, payoff.
- 30-60 seconds: three to five clear beats.
- 60-180 seconds: segment the piece and assign each segment a distinct job.
- Over 3 minutes: add a scene or chapter map before writing shots.

These are planning heuristics, not platform limits. Verify current platform requirements when they affect delivery.
